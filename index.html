Invoice Check

Checks every supplier invoice against its purchase order and what was actually received. Approves the clean ones, and explains each problem in plain words: duplicates, prices above the order, billing for goods that never arrived, items nobody ordered, totals that do not add up.

If checking one invoice by hand takes minutes,

  1. Recognise the vendor. Names are cleaned (case, punctuation, "Ltd", "Inc", "GmbH" style endings) and compared by similarity, so "ACME TOOLS LTD." is read as Acme Tools. Below 75% similar, the vendor is treated as unknown.
  2. Never pay twice. The same vendor with the same invoice digits ("INV-5012", "inv 5012", "Invoice #5012"), or the same vendor, total and order, is blocked as a duplicate.
  3. The invoice must add up. Lines times prices must equal the subtotal, and subtotal plus tax must equal the total (within 0.01 for rounding).
  4. Find the purchase order. First by the order number on the invoice ("PO 1001", "po-1001" and "1001" are read as one). If it is missing or wrong, by the same vendor, an order placed up to 45 days before the invoice, and an order total within 0.5%. If two orders could fit, a person decides.
  5. Compare every line. Each item must be on the order. Quantity billed must not exceed quantity received, adding up all deliveries. Price must not exceed the order price by more than the tolerance.
  6. Decide. Clean invoices are approved. Small price differences inside the tolerance are approved and noted. Everything else goes to a person with the reason and a suggested next step. Nothing is paid or changed by this page.
